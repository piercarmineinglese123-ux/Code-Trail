A **bug** is a defect that makes a program behave differently from what was intended. **Debugging** is the process of finding and removing bugs.

Writing the code usually takes less time than debugging it, so a calm, systematic process is a valuable skill.

## The five steps

1. **Find the root cause.** Where in the code does the real problem start? This is usually the hardest step.
2. **Understand the problem.** Why does the code do this? What did you assume that turned out to be wrong?
3. **Determine a fix.** Decide how to change it properly, not just how to hide the symptom.
4. **Repair the issue.** Make the change, carefully and minimally.
5. **Retest.** Confirm the bug is gone and that you did not break something else.

## Symptoms versus causes

The place where a bug *shows up* is often far from where it *starts*. A wrong total printed at the end may come from a calculation many functions earlier. Patching the visible symptom leaves the cause in place, and the bug returns in another form.

## Good habits

- Reproduce the bug first. A bug you cannot trigger on demand is almost impossible to fix reliably.
- Change one thing at a time, so you know what made the difference.
- After a fix, run your earlier tests again. Fixes can create new bugs.

> [!note] Finding bugs is the heavy lifting. Understanding the cause well usually makes the fix itself small.

## Recap

- A bug is unintended behavior; debugging is finding and fixing it.
- Steps: find the root cause, understand it, decide on a fix, repair, retest.
- Fix causes, not symptoms.
- Always retest after a change.
